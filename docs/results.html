RAG-assisted extraction

92% of cards (11,524) received taxonomy hints from the RAG index during extraction, averaging 458 tokens of context per card with a retrieval latency of 14 ms. The RAG step provides the model with accession-to-taxon mappings from the institution’s database, improving accession number and botanical name accuracy without additional model calls.

Accuracy

Field-level accuracy scoring against a manually reviewed sample has not yet been completed. The earlier prototype (OpenCV alignment + Google Cloud Vision with dynamic template matching) reached roughly 90-98% accuracy on the fields it targeted, useful as a reference point, though it required cloud calls, an internet connection, and far narrower scope.

A formal accuracy assessment is planned: a stratified sample of cards (old vs. new, seed vs. cutting, single vs. multi-sowing) scored against ground-truth transcriptions.

Honest failure modes

Credibility comes from naming what doesn’t work:

  • Condensed multi-sowing tables: the hardest case. Several accessions on one card in a hand-drawn table format. Easy to merge or drop rows. These represent approximately 17% of cards with unknown extraction outcomes.
  • Faded carbon copies: low-contrast old cards (pre-1980s triplicates) degrade transcription quality.
  • Ambiguous handwriting on accession digits: a single misread digit produces a plausible-but-wrong accession. The RAG validation step catches many of these, but not all.
  • No-accession cards: pre-2012 failed sowings rely on taxon-name matching, which inherits any error in the taxon transcription.
  • Family name case inconsistency: the model transcribes family names as written on the card, producing duplicates like “Ericaceae” and “ERICACEAE.” Normalizable in post-processing, but present in the raw output.
